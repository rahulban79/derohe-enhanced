1 · What a contract can and cannot do

It cannot

  • raise or redirect the block reward: it is CalcBlockReward(height) in consensus code;
  • see which address mined a block: no DVM builtin exposes the miner or the integrator;
  • mint DERO: transfers out are checked against the contract's own balance.

It can

  • pay any registered address from DERO it holds, and mint its own token;
  • read the height, the block time, the block id, the signer (ring size 2 only), and draw RANDOM;
  • hold bonds and stakes and release them by rules frozen before anyone joins.

DVM builtins found by the discovery stage (37): address_raw · address_string · assetvalue · atoi · blid · block_height · block_timestamp · delete · dero · derovalue · exists · hex · hexdecode · is_address_valid · itoa · keccak256 · load · mapdelete · mapexists · mapget · mapstore · max · min · panic · random · scid · send_asset_to_address · send_dero_to_address · sha256 · sha3256 · signer · store · strlen · substr · txid · update_sc_code · version. Chain inputs: SCID, BLID, TXID, Signer, BL_HEIGHT, BL_TOPOHEIGHT, BL_TIMESTAMP. A miner can only be counted OFF-chain, from the public block headers.

Does a smart contract need two wallets?

No. Deploying is one transaction from one wallet: the install runs Initialize under that transaction (blockchain/transaction_execute.go:321-354) and the owner is its signer at ring size 2 (:441-447); the other ring member is a random registered account of the chain (walletapi/daemon_communication.go:589-595), not a second wallet of yours. A game between two parties needs two wallets, one per player; a disputed attestation needs a third (a juror may be neither the claimer nor the challenger). The user's mainnet start is therefore two wallets that each hold 4 DERO before the first round.

2 · One block reward, read from the Go code

Live read-only sample of the mainnet node: 60 blocks (heights 7,905,215–7,905,274), mean block interval 17.9 s, difficulty predicted by the code formula in 60/60 blocks, 6.033 distinct normal-miniblock keys per block, fees 0 atomic in the sample. The final miniblock's share and the leftover go to the integrator, the node operator who built the template: a miner who hashes through someone else's node earns nothing for a final miniblock. Side blocks pay nothing, but their normal miniblocks are almost always reused by the winning block, so the orphan cost falls on the integrator.

4 · The mechanisms

M1 · Miner-attested token (MAT): optimistic bonded claim, challenge, commit-reveal jurors GO

Contract gametheory/contracts/gt_attest.bas · entrypoints Initialize, SetParams, Freeze, Claim, Challenge, Stake, Unstake, CommitVote, RevealVote, Finalize, ClaimJuror

Equilibrium. Truthful claiming is the attractor iff bond > (1-c) * reward * v / c per miniblock, c = P(challenge) * P(jury correct) (econsim m1_critical_bond, replicator). Jurors: coherent voting earns a share of the loser's bond plus slashed stakes; incoherent or unrevealed jurors lose ceil(slash %) of their stake (Schelling point).

Sustainability. MAT is issued, not bought: its value is what fee payers and sponsors in M2/M3 pay for the boost. Bonds and slashes are zero-sum inside one dispute. Needs at least one honest verifier-challenger and juror participation (no reveal = claim upheld).

Attack surface.

  • sybil: a miner may split into many addresses; claims are per address and per height range, so splitting gains nothing
  • collusion / P+epsilon bribe of jurors (econsim m1_bribe_cost); mitigation: commit-reveal, slashing, stake caps; residual risk stated
  • griefing: a challenger loses its bond to an upheld honest claim
  • front-running: votes are committed as sha256(i:vote:salt); claims and challenges are public by design
  • gas DoS: no loops; every entrypoint O(1)

M2 · Useful service (notary) with a bounded fee rebate and a MAT boost GO

Contract gametheory/contracts/gt_service.bas · entrypoints Initialize, SetParams, SetMAT, Freeze, Notarize, Sponsor, StakeMAT, UnstakeMAT, ClaimRebate, WithdrawTreasury

Equilibrium. No profitable wash trading iff rho * (100 + boost) <= 10000 (enforced in SetParams; econsim m2_wash_profitable grid). A sponsor subsidy S is a Tullock contest: equilibrium fee spend X* = S (n-1) / (n (1-rho)); the subsidy is dissipated, a fraction (n-1)/n going to the treasury as fees.

Sustainability. Rebates come only from the same epoch's fees and sponsor funds; pools close at the epoch end. A sponsor buys activity, not organic demand: subsidised activity must be reported as subsidised (NO-GO N3).

Attack surface.

  • wash trading (bounded as above)
  • MAT recycling across addresses: locked until the epoch end
  • first-writer-wins front-running of a document digest: the digest is public once sent

M3 · No-loss prize pool with a commit-reveal draw and a MAT boost GO

Contract gametheory/contracts/gt_prize.bas · entrypoints Initialize, SetParams, SetMAT, Freeze, StakeMAT, UnstakeMAT, Fund, Enter, Reveal, Draw, Withdraw

Equilibrium. Entry EV = prize * w / W - fees >= 0 (principal is returned); the MAT boost doubles a miner's weight. Withholding is unprofitable while prize <= rbond * (n_revealed - 1) (last revealer). v2 (D-GT-10) draws from the revealed seed only: v1 mixed RANDOM (keyed by the block id), which let the Draw block's integrator withhold the block to re-roll at the cost of one integrator share (3,075 atomic on mainnet today); econsim m3_withholding.

Sustainability. Prizes are sponsor funds plus reveal bonds forfeited in the same round; never another participant's principal.

Attack surface.

  • last-revealer withholding (bond-bounded)
  • v1 only: integrator block withholding to re-roll RANDOM (removed in v2)
  • reveal censorship: an integrator can leave a reveal tx out of its block, but the reveal window spans many blocks and integrators (12 distinct integrators in 60 live blocks, reward_analysis)
  • gas DoS: maxn <= 64 entries per round, the v2 Draw loop measured at 1,774,800 compute gas for 32 entries of the 10,000,000 limit
